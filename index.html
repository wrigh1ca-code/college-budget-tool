5Your net worth

Your net worth is everything you own minus everything you owe. It's the simplest way to see your whole financial picture in one number. The cash in your checking and savings, minus what you've set aside for tuition, is what you'll use to pay for moving in.

Why it matters: your monthly budget shows whether you're spending less than you make. Net worth shows whether that's actually building something over time. If your net worth goes up every few months, your plan is working.

Update these numbers every three months or so, and write down the date and total. Only count a car if it's in your name, and use its private-party value from a site like Kelley Blue Book (kbb.com). In YNAB, you can add your car, investments and loans as tracking accounts to see your net worth there too.

From this plan to YNAB

This page builds your plan: a monthly budget that includes the apartment. YNAB is where you follow that plan each month after you move. The groups here match YNAB category groups, and each line becomes a YNAB category with that amount as its monthly target.

Set up YNAB from this budget

Create a category group for each group here, and a category for each line. Set each category's target to the amount shown in the table. Copy the list so you have it next to you while you set up YNAB.

Record housing assistance as income

When housing assistance lands in your account, record it as income and assign it, the same as a paycheck. Transportation costs that are covered for you stay out of your YNAB budget. If you ever get money for them instead, record it as income and add those car categories.

Change your targets when summer starts

YNAB targets are monthly. When summer starts, switch each category to its summer amount, and switch back when school starts again. Assign your extra summer income to your tuition fund and savings first. That's the money that gets you through the tighter school months.

Fund the costs that come once or twice a year

YNAB calls these true expenses. They include tuition, textbooks, car repairs and next year's deposit. Funding them a little each month means the money is there when the bill comes. Put lump sums like financial aid or a tax refund toward these categories first.

Compare with real spending every month for the first three months

At the end of each month, look at what YNAB shows you actually spent and update the amounts here. Groceries, eating out and utilities are the numbers that usually miss. If what's left at the end of the month goes negative, you'll see it before it turns into credit card debt.

Update this page when something changes

A raise, a new roommate, a lease renewal or a change in what's covered: change it here first and check that the budget still works. Then update your targets in YNAB.
